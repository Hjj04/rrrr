02 / Abstract

Abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation models generalize across a broad range of manipulation tasks, yet reliable real-world deployment often requires further adaptation to task-specific dynamics. We study two complementary post-training settings: rapid residual adaptation of independently task-tuned policies and consolidation of task-specific interaction experience into one shared multi-task model. To this end, we propose RRRR (Rapid Real-World Residual RL), a plug-and-play framework that couples task-specific residual adaptation of frozen VLA backbones with residual-guided consolidation of successful rollouts into a standalone shared VLA.

For task-specific adaptation, a high-frequency residual policy provides closed-loop corrections to low-frequency VLA action chunks, is optimized with a unified imitation learning (IL) and reinforcement learning (RL) objective, and can incorporate auxiliary feedback such as force feedback and estimated depth maps. For shared multi-task consolidation, task-specific residual policies are trained on a frozen mixed-task SFT backbone, and the executed residual scales recorded along successful rollouts are used as sample-level weights when fine-tuning the shared VLA.

On four real-world manipulation tasks, residual adaptation raises the average success rates of task-specific OpenVLA-OFT and π0.5 backbones from 0.00% and 45.00% to 91.25%, respectively; the evaluated checkpoints are reached after 3.15–27.30 min of residual training in our setup. Weighted consolidation attains 85.00% average success, compared with 23.75% when the same rollouts are trained with uniform weights. On a custom 25-task LIBERO joint-training setting, RRRR reaches 91.80% average success, exceeding two representative RL-based data-generation baselines by 6.64 and 4.28 percentage points.

03 / Framework

From residual correction to a shared VLA

RRRR treats real-world adaptation as residual compensation rather than further modification of the base model. One mechanism serves two settings: correcting a VLA that was fine-tuned for a single task, and folding the experience of many such corrections back into one shared multi-task VLA.

I

Per-task residual adaptation

Freeze a VLA that was fine-tuned for one task. Train a lightweight residual actor–critic around it on the real robot, guided first by demonstrations and human corrections, then increasingly by RL.

II

Rollout collection

On one shared VLA trained on mixed-task demonstrations, run each task's base + residual policy. Keep the successful rollouts and the residual scale that was executed at every step.

III

Residual-guided weighted SFT

Turn the recorded scales into curriculum weights and fine-tune the shared VLA on its own successful rollouts. The result is one standalone VLA that needs no residual at inference.

Technical formulationControl, optimization & curriculum weighting

Fast–slow residual control

Within an action chunk, a VLA runs open loop: execution errors accumulate and contact disturbances go unanswered until the next inference. RRRR splits inference into two synchronized paths. The slow path queries the frozen VLA at about 10 Hz for a chunk of \(N\) actions and caches them in a FIFO queue. At every control step, roughly \(N\) times faster, the fast path pops the current base action and a lightweight MLP adds a residual correction, scaled by the dynamic residual scale:

\[ a_t \;=\; \class{cb}{a_{\mathrm{base},t}} \;+\; \class{cs}{\bar{\Lambda}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{act}}}\, \class{cr}{a_{\mathrm{res},t}}, \qquad \class{cr}{a_{\mathrm{res},t}} = \pi\big(\mathcal{E}(s_t,\, a_{\mathrm{base},t},\, n);\, \theta_i\big) \tag{3} \]

The residual sees the base action and the intra-chunk index \(n\), so it knows how far the current chunk has progressed. Depth maps (estimated from RGB with Depth Anything V2) and force–torque readings feed only the residual path; the base VLA keeps its native observation interface.

Dynamic residual scale

The scale decides how much of the residual reaches the robot. After each step it is updated from two signals, clipped to \([0, \lambda_{\max}]\), and smoothed. The critic first scores the action built with the current scale, and only then is the scale for the next step computed, which avoids a circular dependence.

Candidate-value modulation. The critic compares the scaled candidate against the base action alone. Only a positive estimated gain opens this gate.

\[ A_i^{\mathrm{cand}}(s_t) = Q_{\phi_i}(s_t, a_{\mathrm{cand},t}) - Q_{\phi_i}(s_t, \class{cb}{a_{\mathrm{base},t}}) \tag{4} \]
\[ s_{\mathrm{value},t} = 2\max\!\Big(0,\ \operatorname{sigmoid}\!\big(A_i^{\mathrm{cand}}(s_t)/T\big) - 0.5\Big) \tag{5} \]

Stagnation escape. A learned critic can make that gate overly conservative. When batch-level critic progress over the last \(W\) learner updates falls below its moving reference \(\hat g_u\), the escape signal turns positive and strengthens correction exactly when learning slows.

\[ g_u = \bar{Q}_u - \bar{Q}_{u-W}, \qquad s_{\mathrm{escape},u} = \max\!\big(0,\ \max(0, \hat{g}_u) - g_u\big) \tag{6–8} \]
\[ \class{cs}{\Lambda_{i,t+1}^{\mathrm{raw}}} = \operatorname{clip}\!\big(\alpha_v\, s_{\mathrm{value},t} + \alpha_e\, s_{\mathrm{escape},u(t)},\ 0,\ \lambda_{\max}\big) \tag{9} \]
\[ \class{cs}{\bar{\Lambda}_{i,t+1}^{\mathrm{act}}} = \eta_{\Lambda}\, \class{cs}{\Lambda_{i,t+1}^{\mathrm{raw}}} + (1-\eta_{\Lambda})\, \class{cs}{\bar{\Lambda}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{act}}} \tag{10} \]

The scale executed at each step is logged with the transition. Stage III reuses it as a per-transition record of how strongly the residual had to intervene.

Unified IL–RL optimization

The residual actor blends imitation of human corrections with Soft Actor-Critic. The imitation weight \(\rho(t)\) starts at 1, which acts as pure behavior cloning and an implicit actor initialization, then follows a cosine decay to \(\rho_{\min}\).

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{res}}(\theta_i) = \rho(t)\, \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{IL}} + \big(1-\rho(t)\big)\, \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SAC}} \tag{11} \]
\[ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{IL}} = \mathbb{E}\Big[\big\lVert \class{cs}{\bar{\Lambda}_{i}^{\mathrm{act}}(s)}\, \class{cr}{\pi(s;\theta_i)} - \delta^{*} \big\rVert_2^2\Big], \qquad \delta^{*} = a_h - \class{cb}{\mu(s;\Phi_{\mathrm{base}})} \tag{12} \]

The expert buffer holds teleoperated demonstrations and online human-in-the-loop correction frames, which supervise exactly the failure-prone states the current policy reaches. Before online training, the critic is warmed up offline with Cal-QL. Online batches mix 50% online and 50% expert transitions in the style of RLPD, with an update-to-data ratio of 4 and DrQ-style image augmentation. The reward is a sparse success bonus with a step penalty, \(r_t = \mathbf{1}[\text{success}] - c\). Actor updates lock after \(K_{\text{lock}}\) consecutive autonomous successes once Q-value improvement drops below \(\epsilon_Q\).

Multimodal residual encoder

Four streams are encoded and each is modulated by the chunk-progress embedding through FiLM, with a residual connection and LayerNorm, before late fusion into a 512-dimensional latent \(\mathbf{z}_t\).

Chunk progress
\(\mathbf{f}_{\text{step}} = \mathrm{MLP}([\mathrm{PE}(n) \parallel n/N])\), a sinusoidal encoding of the intra-chunk index. It is also the FiLM anchor for the other streams.
Geometric intention
The offset \(\Delta p_n\) between the base action's target position and the current end-effector, encoded as direction, magnitude and raw offset.
Force–torque
A mean-centered high-frequency force–torque history, processed by a 1D-CNN.
Vision
RGB-D through ResNet-18 with CoordConv, where depth is estimated from RGB by Depth Anything V2.

Residual-guided weighted SFT

Each successful composed-policy rollout keeps the scale \(\lambda^{\mathrm{exec}}\) actually executed at every transition. Per task, the scales are normalized by the 20th and 80th percentiles, so the central range maps to \([0,1]\) and both tails clip.

\[ \lambda_{i,k}^{\mathrm{norm}} = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{\lambda_{i,k}^{\mathrm{exec}} - q_{20,i}}{q_{80,i} - q_{20,i} + \epsilon},\ 0,\ 1\right) \tag{20} \]

A Gaussian kernel centered at a curriculum peak \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{peak}}(v)\) turns each normalized scale into a weight. The peak rises monotonically over training: the shared model first learns from transitions that needed little correction, then shifts toward states where the residual intervened strongly.

\[ \omega_{i,k}^{\mathrm{raw}} = \exp\!\left(-\frac{\big(\lambda_{i,k}^{\mathrm{norm}} - \class{cs}{\Lambda_{\mathrm{peak}}(v)}\big)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right), \qquad \omega'_{i,k} = \min(\omega_{i,k}^{\mathrm{raw}}, \omega_{\max}) \tag{21} \]

How the curriculum reweights a batch

Task-balanced transitions, weighted by normalized executed residual scale.

Illustration with synthetic samples. The 20/80-percentile clipping places about a fifth of each task at 0 and at 1. The linear peak schedule and the σ range shown here are for illustration, not values reported in the paper.

Batches draw \(B/M\) samples per task, and weights are renormalized to mean one within each batch to keep gradient magnitudes consistent. The shared VLA is then fine-tuned with weighted flow matching on the executed actions:

\[ \hat{\omega}_b = \frac{\omega'_b}{\frac{1}{B}\sum_{j=1}^{B}\omega'_j + \epsilon}, \qquad \mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{b\sim\mathcal{B}}\Big[\hat{\omega}_b\, \big\lVert v_{\Phi_{\mathrm{vla}}}(x_u, u, c) - v^{*}(x_u, a_{\mathrm{final}}) \big\rVert_2^2\Big] \tag{22–23} \]

Depth, force, the residual policy and the recorded scale are used only to generate and weight the data. The consolidated VLA runs on its native RGB, robot state and language inputs.

04 / Real-world evaluation

Rapid single-task adaptation

Four Franka tasks test adaptation: Weight Insertion, Tissue Extraction, Balance Placement and Toy Suspension. Each task-specific backbone is LoRA-fine-tuned on 20 teleoperated demonstrations. A lightweight binary reward model, trained on human-labeled rollouts, supplies both the sparse training reward and automatic success evaluation.

Table I. Success rate over 20 trials per task. Time is residual-training wall-clock time to the evaluated checkpoint.

Task LoRA-SFT VLA + Residual Δ Time (min)

On OpenVLA-OFT, the LoRA-SFT policies fail every trial, and the residual raises the average to 91.25%. Weight Insertion and Tissue Extraction reach 100% after 3.15 and 4.35 minutes of residual learning. On π0.5, which already handles some tasks, the residual still lifts Weight Insertion from 5% to 100% and Toy Suspension from 10% to 70%.

Imitation guidance supports reliable learning. Training with RL alone reduces success by 33.3 and 36.7 percentage points. Removing the stagnation-escape term also lowers success on both tasks.

Dynamic scaling improves control stability. A fixed scale stays near saturation on Weight Insertion but drops Pick-and-Place to 73.3%, and the spread of execution time grows from ±0.83 s to ±19.36 s.

Auxiliary modalities improve execution efficiency. Without force or depth inputs, success stays at 93.3–100%, but execution takes 9–47% longer. Every ablation increases mean completion time.

06 / Multi-task learning

One shared VLA for many tasks

Real-world Franka

A shared π0.5 is first trained on 80 mixed demonstrations, 20 per task. Three adaptations are compared against it. Base + Residuali keeps the shared VLA frozen and runs a separate residual per task at inference. Weighted SFT fine-tunes the shared VLA on 80 successful composed-policy rollouts with residual-scale weights. Without weighting uses exactly the same rollouts and settings with uniform weights.

LIBERO, 25 tasks trained jointly

The benchmark takes all 10 long-horizon tasks of LIBERO-10 and a fixed 15-task subset of LIBERO-90 from three scenes: Kitchen (6), Living Room (5) and Study (4). All methods share the π0.5 backbone, task split, optimization schedule and evaluation protocol, and every data-generation method gets the same budget of 50 generated trajectories per task. DPRL generates training data with diffusion-based RL; PLD uses a state-independent residual bound and standard unweighted SFT. RLDG is not evaluated because its specialist stage needs real-robot human corrections outside the LIBERO protocol.

07 / Transfer

A second embodiment

On the ZERITH H1 wheeled bimanual platform, snack retrieval chains grasping, handover and placement: the right arm takes a snack from the shelf, passes it to the left arm, and the left arm puts it in a basket. The base VLA stays frozen and only the task-specific residual is trained, under the same action composition.

Across 20 trials, success rises from 0% with the frozen base VLA to 75% (15/20) with the residual. This is a single-task result and provides preliminary evidence that the residual mechanism carries over beyond the single-arm Franka setup without updating the base VLA.

08 / Scope

Limitations

The real-world study covers four primary Franka tasks, and the single-task H1 experiment is preliminary cross-embodiment evidence. Reported training times measure wall-clock adaptation speed rather than environment-step sample efficiency, and Table I does not compare against direct online optimization of the VLA parameters. Future work will look at shared residual representations, broader embodiments, and adaptation with less task-specific interaction.
